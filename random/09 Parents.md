If your children commit to being startup founders

Know that there is nothing fun about it

To truly startup, is to put everything else in their life second to making progress on their business

The only thing between them and success is a mile-long list of crucial lessons

At any age, understand that they are committing to competing against impossibly large incumbents

They are committing to months of eating ramen while they "figure things out"

The problems they face will span the entire range of what makes a business succeed from marketing, product, and sales

It is not "for fun" or for the flex of being a "startup founder"

It is for money, and hypothesized market dominance

They must feel they have spotted a slice of opportunity, and have made the decision to go all in

If they have brains, and have considered their personal life position and still think they have a chance, they must be bold, and have a somewhat technical understanding of the space they are devoting to work within

On the contrary to your doubts, doubts they will have already processed and overcome, all wealth creation in the US over the last few decades has been through startups

Support your local entrepeneur

Motivate them to get more technical with their services, they can do it, and it will scale beyond their hours

I wish my parents had supported me! I'm doing this for them! Not to get out of anything! I'm signing up for much more of a tumultuous journey of starting from nothing than is offered as a member of someone else's staff!

I would say to parents who struggle to internally feel comfortable with such risks of entrepreneurship, to learn about who started the company you work for and to look to get inspired. It must have actually happened right? Or how are you getting such a reliable check!

This reliability in business is what your child is working towards, and is what that intangible understanding of the market provides. The developed awareness of customer needs and market-impacting factors truly helps them prepare for future trends.

All startup founders are hoping to grow, increase profits, and win more customers.

If they are stupid, they usually are, go ahead and debate them as a shareholder cofounder. What are they overlooking? They should be able to provide strong arguments for why they are building the plausible solution. Approach them as a customer, how are they better than this obvious alternative?

This is the craft they are committing to. Sales is a big part of it. If they are to succeed, it should be blatantly obvious why you would make the decision to pay them instead of some competing service.
